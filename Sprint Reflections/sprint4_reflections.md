This sprint was one of the more technically hands-on experiences I've had in this project, and it pushed me to learn things I hadn't anticipated going in. The most significant learning moment came from working with SVG files and figuring out how to properly import custom icons into our website. What seemed like a straightforward task, converting an image into an SVG and dropping it into the codebase, turned out to be far more nuanced. I learned the hard way that most online JPG-to-SVG converters don't actually produce real vector files; they simply wrap image data inside an SVG tag, which renders as nothing when used as an icon. Working through this problem taught me to always verify SVG files using the terminal, to source proper vector files from sites like svgrepo.com, and to understand how properties like currentColor allow icons to inherit a site's theme seamlessly. It was a small detail with a big impact, and understanding it gave me much more confidence working with frontend assets going forward.

Alongside this, I completed the documentation for our icons folder, and through that process I found myself thinking about README files and technical documentation very differently than before. I used to see documentation as something you wrote after the fact, a summary of what was already done. But writing it this sprint, especially having to explain not just what each file does but why certain decisions were made, made me realize that good documentation is really a form of design thinking. It also gave me a clearer picture of where this project is heading, and honestly, I'm excited about it. There are possibilities here that go beyond what I initially imagined, and I find myself looking forward to what people in next semester can build next.

In terms of team dynamics, compared to the beginning of the semester, the way we collaborate now feels noticeably more connected and in sync. I've been making a conscious effort to communicate more with each team member individually, and I can feel that it's made a difference. One thing that shifted my perspective this sprint was our update routine, sharing progress at weekly meetings and before each group session. I'll be honest: I used to find it repetitive and a little unnecessary. But watching my teammates consistently show up prepared and hold each other accountable made me realize how much that rhythm actually matters. Knowing what everyone else is working on, in real time, keeps the team moving in the same direction and prevents the kind of miscommunication that can quietly derail a project. It's something I now genuinely value rather than just tolerate. I love and also learn a lot from this type of pre-group meeting session.
